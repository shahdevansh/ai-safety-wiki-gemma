# Assistance Games

The lecture notes frame assistance as promoting human interests while remaining uncertain about what those interests are. They discuss inferring interests from behavior and mental states, and warn that observed preferences can be manipulated. This is a summary of the lecture's framing, not proof that the approach solves alignment.

## Useful details

- The notes give different reasons for preferring “interests”: “values” prompted culture-war reactions, while stated preferences can be shaped against a person's interests.
- Behavior alone can leave beliefs and preferences indistinguishable. The proposed inference uses Bayesian priors; idealized convergence assumes infinite data and a fixed model of human decision-making.
- Preference plasticity, manipulation and conflicting interests across people remain difficulties.
- The mountain-climbing example distinguishes reaching an outcome from exercising agency in the process. The notes propose including mental states and process in the preference model.
- The lecture identifies a training-data gap: observed human behavior does not itself supply labeled optimal assistance-game solutions.
